Whoever controls a person's email can reset the passwords for their banking, shopping, and photos. So email comes first, then recovery.

## A password manager for the family

A password manager makes a different strong password for every site, fills it in, and flags reused or leaked ones. A family plan gives each person a private vault, plus shared ones.

- **Good options:** 1Password Families, Bitwarden Families (with a free individual tier), and Apple Passwords or Google Password Manager if your household is all on one platform.
- **Shared vaults** for the Wi-Fi, streaming, utilities, and the household tech sheet. Everything else is private, teenagers included.
- **An emergency kit**, printed and kept safe, in case the main password is forgotten.

Set it up for yourself first, then help one person at a time, starting with email and bank.

More: [Let a password manager remember for you](https://github.com/neon1llc/ready-secure-go/blob/main/content/practices/accounts-and-identity/acct-password-manager.md).

## Two-step sign-in

Turn it on for email first, then banking, then the family Apple or Google account. Passkeys are strongest and easiest where offered. Text message codes are fine for people who find apps hard.

**Never read a sign-in code to anyone who asks.** Real companies don't.

## Recovery that works

For each person's email, check:

- The recovery phone and email are current and ones they can still use.
- Backup codes are in the password manager or printed and kept safe.
- Apple accounts have a recovery contact. Google accounts have current options on the Security page.

For shared services, use Apple Family Sharing, Google family groups, or streaming profiles instead of one shared login. You can add and remove people without changing a password.
