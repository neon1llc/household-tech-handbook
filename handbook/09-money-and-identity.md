Identity thieves often use information that already leaked in a breach. You can't take it back, but you can make it hard to use.

## Freeze everyone's credit

A credit freeze stops anyone, including you, from opening new credit in your name until you lift it. It's free in the United States, doesn't affect your credit score, and takes about fifteen minutes per person.

- Freeze with all three bureaus: **Equifax**, **Experian**, and **TransUnion**, through their websites or apps.
- **Children too.** A child's clean record is valuable to thieves, and theft can go unnoticed for years. Parents can request a free freeze for a child under 16 through each bureau's process for minors.
- Save each bureau's login or PIN in the password manager. To apply for credit, lift the freeze briefly, then restore it.

## Bank and card controls

- **Alerts** on every transaction, or every one over a small amount.
- **Card controls** in the bank's app: lock a misplaced card, block international use, or set limits.
- **Virtual card numbers** for online shopping, where offered.
- **Two-step sign-in** on every financial account.

## A yearly look

- Check each adult's credit reports at **AnnualCreditReport.com**, where they're free once a week from each bureau.
- Check your email addresses against known breaches with Have I Been Pwned or your password manager's breach report, and change affected passwords.
- Consider removing your address and phone number from data broker sites, by hand or with a paid service.
