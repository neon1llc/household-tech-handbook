Most households have old tablets in drawers, a smart TV signed in to three streaming services, and an email address from 2009 that still gets the bank's statements.

## What to write down

The [household tech sheet](../templates/household-tech-sheet.md) has room for:

1. **People.** Everyone in the household, plus anyone you help, such as parents.
2. **The internet.** Provider, account holder, router, and where its admin login is kept.
3. **Devices.** Anything on the Wi-Fi. The [device inventory](../templates/device-inventory.csv) is a spreadsheet version.
4. **Key accounts.** Main email, the Apple or Google family group, the password manager, banking, and bills.
5. **Who's the admin** of each, able to reset passwords and add or remove people.

## What to look for

- **Shared accounts tied to one person's email.** Only that person can fix them.
- **Devices nobody claims.** Wipe and retire them ([chapter 10](10-buying-and-retiring.md)).
- **Recovery details that point nowhere**, such as a phone number given up years ago.

## Where to keep it

As a secure note in your password manager shared with one other adult, or printed and stored safely. It holds no passwords.

## Do this

- [ ] Household tech sheet filled in.
- [ ] Every shared account marked, with who controls it.
- [ ] A second adult knows where the sheet is.
