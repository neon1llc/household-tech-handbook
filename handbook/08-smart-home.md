description: "Buy cameras, doorbells, speakers, and plugs that get updates, set them up safely, and keep them off your main network."

The risky smart devices are cheap, rarely updated, and still on the factory password.

## Before you buy

- Choose brands that publish how long they'll provide security updates and support two-step sign-in.
- Look for Matter, a standard that works across brands. The U.S. Cyber Trust Mark label will help once labeled products reach stores, which the FCC was still working toward in late 2026.
- Skip devices that need an account with an unknown company or an app with poor reviews.

## Setting up

1. Put the device on the **guest or devices network** ([chapter 4](04-router-and-wifi.md)).
2. Change any default password, and turn on two-step sign-in for the app's account.
3. Turn on automatic firmware updates.
4. Turn off what you won't use: remote access, microphones, and data sharing.

## Cameras and voice

- Keep indoor cameras out of bedrooms and bathrooms, and off or covered when you're home.
- Use end-to-end encryption if offered, and check who can see the feeds.
- Keep cameras off neighbors' homes, and check local laws on recording audio.
- On voice assistants and smart TVs, turn off recording history, human review of recordings, and viewing data sharing (often called ACR or "viewing information").

More: [secure your cameras and smart devices](https://github.com/neon1llc/ready-secure-go/blob/main/content/practices/devices-and-updates/dev-iot-and-cameras.md).

## Do this

- [ ] Every smart device on the guest network, with default passwords changed.
- [ ] Automatic updates and two-step sign-in on.
- [ ] Camera access and privacy settings reviewed.
