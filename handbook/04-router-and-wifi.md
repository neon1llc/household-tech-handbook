Every device in the house reaches the internet through the router, and in many homes nobody has signed in to it since it was installed.

## Find it and sign in

1. Find the router. If your internet provider supplied it, it may be a combined modem and router.
2. Look for the admin address and login on its label or in the provider's app.
3. Sign in and **change the admin password** (and the admin username, if it lets you) to a long, unique one stored in your password manager. If the label's password still works, anyone who has seen it can change your settings.

## The settings that matter

- **Firmware updates.** Turn on automatic updates if offered. If the router hasn't had an update in years, it's time to replace it.
- **Wi-Fi security.** Choose WPA3, or WPA2/WPA3 mixed mode if older devices need it. WPA2 is acceptable if that's the best the router offers. Never "open", WEP, or the original WPA.
- **The Wi-Fi password.** Long and unique. Four or five unrelated words work well.
- **Remote management** off, unless you use the provider's app on purpose.
- **WPS** (push-button pairing) off, unless a device needs it.
- **UPnP** off, unless something such as a game console needs it.

## A guest network for guests and gadgets

Most routers can run a second network. Put visitors on it, along with smart TVs, speakers, plugs, and cameras. A hacked gadget then can't reach your laptops and phones.

## Mesh systems and coverage

For weak Wi-Fi upstairs, a mesh system (several units working together) usually beats a range extender. Mesh systems from major brands also tend to update themselves.

## Do this

- [ ] Router admin password changed and saved in the password manager.
- [ ] Firmware current, with automatic updates on.
- [ ] WPA3 or WPA2/WPA3, with a strong Wi-Fi password.
- [ ] A guest network for visitors and smart devices.
- [ ] Router details recorded on the household tech sheet.
