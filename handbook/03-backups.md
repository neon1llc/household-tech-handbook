A dead laptop can take years of photos with it if they exist nowhere else.

## The 3-2-1 idea

Keep **three** copies of anything you can't replace, on **two** kinds of storage, with **one** away from home:

1. The original.
2. A cloud copy: iCloud Photos, Google Photos, OneDrive, or Dropbox.
3. A second copy you control, such as an external drive or another cloud service. Unplug the drive between backups, or keep older versions, so ransomware or a mistake can't wipe every copy.

## What to back up

- **Phones:** iCloud Backup or Google One backup, plus photo sync. Backups stop when cloud storage is full.
- **Computers:** Time Machine (Mac) or File History (Windows) to an external drive, plus a cloud backup.
- **Photos:** one shared family album, not six phones.
- **Documents:** scans of IDs, insurance, property, and medical records, in your password manager or an encrypted cloud folder.

## Test a restore

A backup you haven't restored from is a guess. Once a year, restore a few photos and a document to a different device and note the date.

More: [the 3-2-1 way](https://github.com/neon1llc/ready-secure-go/blob/main/content/practices/data-and-backups/data-3-2-1-backup.md) and [prove your backup works](https://github.com/neon1llc/ready-secure-go/blob/main/content/practices/data-and-backups/data-test-a-restore.md).

## Do this

- [ ] Phone and computer backups running, with enough storage.
- [ ] Documents scanned and stored encrypted.
- [ ] A restore tested, with the date on the household tech sheet.
