## Before you buy

The spec nobody prints on the box is **how many years of security updates** the device gets.

- **Phones:** Google gives Pixel 8 and later seven years of OS and security updates from when each model went on sale. Samsung offers seven years on recent flagship Galaxy phones. Apple doesn't publish a fixed period. Cheaper Android phones may get far fewer, so check the maker's support page.
- **Computers:** check the model can run the current Windows or macOS, and how long that version is supported.
- **Smart devices and routers:** check the maker publishes an update period ([chapter 8](08-smart-home.md)).
- **Secondhand:** confirm the previous owner removed it from their account (Activation Lock on Apple devices, Factory Reset Protection on Android), or you can't use it.

## Retiring a device

Before you sell, donate, trade in, or recycle anything:

1. **Back it up** ([chapter 3](03-backups.md)).
2. **Sign out** of the Apple, Google, or Microsoft account. On Apple devices, turning off Find My removes Activation Lock.
3. **Unpair** watches and accessories.
4. **Factory reset.** On modern encrypted devices, a reset makes the old data unrecoverable.
5. **Remove it** from your account's device list and the [device inventory](../templates/device-inventory.csv).
6. **Remove SIM and memory cards.**

Old unencrypted hard drives need the operating system's secure erase, or destruction. Use trade-in programs or certified e-waste recyclers, not household trash.

More: [retire devices that can't get updates](https://github.com/neon1llc/ready-secure-go/blob/main/content/practices/devices-and-updates/dev-retire-old-devices.md).

## Do this

- [ ] Check the update period before every purchase.
- [ ] Retire any device that no longer gets updates.
- [ ] Follow the six steps before a device leaves the house.
