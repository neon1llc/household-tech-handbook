A child who can report something strange without losing their phone will tell you sooner. The conversation matters as much as the settings.

## Set up through a family account

Create the child's account inside your family group, not as a standalone one. These cover purchase approval, age ratings, screen time, and location sharing:

- **Apple:** Family Sharing, then Screen Time.
- **Google and Android:** Family Link.
- **Microsoft, Xbox, and Windows:** Family Safety.
- **Nintendo and PlayStation:** their parental controls.

## By age, roughly

| Age | A starting point |
|---|---|
| Under 8 | Shared family tablet in common rooms, with apps you've chosen. No open browser or chat. |
| 8 to 12 | Own tablet or basic phone. Purchase approval on, overnight downtime, chat with approved contacts only. |
| 13 to 15 | A smartphone with limits you agree on and loosen as trust grows. Social media only at the platform's minimum age, with private profiles. |
| 16 and up | Most controls off. Keep two-step sign-in, find my device, and the agreement that they can always come to you. |

## The conversations

- **Anyone online who asks for secrets or photos is not a friend.** Tell a parent and you won't be in trouble.
- **Never share sign-in codes or passwords**, even with friends. Gaming account theft is common.
- **Free in-game currency, "skins," and gift cards** are common money scams.
- **Sextortion:** if anyone threatens to share pictures, block them, keep the messages, and tell a parent. Paying rarely stops it. [Take It Down](https://takeitdown.ncmec.org/) can help remove images of minors.

More: [the family tech talk](https://github.com/neon1llc/ready-secure-go/blob/main/content/practices/people-and-culture/ppl-family-tech-talk.md) and [a kid's in trouble online](https://github.com/neon1llc/ready-secure-go/blob/main/content/playbooks/kid-in-trouble-online.md).

## Do this

- [ ] Each child's account inside the family group, with age-appropriate controls.
- [ ] Two-step sign-in where the account allows.
