---
title: Visiting parents checklist
description: "About two hours, in person, to make a parent's devices simpler, safer, and easier to help from far away."
---
Go at their pace, explain each change, and ask first. The reasons are in [chapter 7](../handbook/07-aging-parents.md).

## Phone

- [ ] Updates installed, automatic updates on.
- [ ] Larger and bold text, and any accessibility features they like.
- [ ] Your number in favorites, with doctors and family saved as contacts.
- [ ] Unknown callers silenced or screened.
- [ ] Find My on, and location shared with you if they agree.
- [ ] Backups and photo sync on, with enough storage.

## Accounts and money

- [ ] Two-step sign-in on email, using the method they find easiest.
- [ ] Recovery phone and email current, backup codes saved somewhere safe.
- [ ] A password manager, or the phone's built-in one, set up for their most-used accounts.
- [ ] Legacy and recovery contacts set ([chapter 11](../handbook/11-emergencies-and-legacy.md)).
- [ ] Bank alerts for large withdrawals, and a trusted contact named if the bank offers it.
- [ ] Credit freezes at all three bureaus.

## Computer and home

- [ ] Updates, screen lock, and built-in antivirus on.
- [ ] Router admin password changed and firmware current, if they have their own internet.
- [ ] Remote screen sharing tested together.

## Conversation

- [ ] Hang up, look up, call back.
- [ ] A family code word for emergency calls.
- [ ] "Call me first" for any request for money, gift cards, or remote access.
- [ ] [Phishing Awareness Kit](https://github.com/neon1llc/phishing-awareness-kit) takeaway left by the phone.
